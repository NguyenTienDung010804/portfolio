03  What I realised

At first, I read the views as proof we were on the right path. It took many unsold pieces to see what the numbers were really saying: people liked watching our story, but very few wanted to wear our clothes.

We had designed around our own preferences and ideals, and never truly asked:

  • What do customers actually want to wear?
  • What are they willing to pay for?
  • Why would they choose us over what they already have?

Sustainability alone was not a reason to buy. A broad ambition like "sustainable, gender-inclusive fashion" has to be narrowed into a specific customer problem before it can become a useful, viable product.

02  Key contributions
①  Simplifying debt collection
INSIGHT

We first focused on the dashboard. But merchants said collecting each debt was the real pain:

  1. Remind the customer, again and again
  2. Send bank details and the amount
  3. Check the bank app: has it arrived?

Example: a wife owed the debt, but her husband paid. The bank showed his name, not hers.

Accounting lens: the hard part is not asking for money, but matching each transfer to the right debt.

SOLUTION

Instead of adding more dashboard features, I advocated prioritising the QR debt-collection flow: a QR request linked to each debt, pre-filled with the amount, bank details and a clear payment reference, shareable via any messaging app, so every transfer can be checked against the right debt account.

IMPACT

Merchants responded very positively to how convenient it was: fewer manual messages, no typing for customers, and easier payment matching.

②  Ensuring payment accuracy
INSIGHT

Merchants marked debts as paid from a transfer screenshot, before money arrived. From an audit perspective, reporting, receiving and allocating a payment are distinct events; recording them as one creates a risk of misstated balances.

SOLUTION

Distinct payment states, reported, verified, then allocated, with business rules, acceptance criteria and test cases for partial, duplicate and reversed payments.

IMPACT

Balances that reflect money actually received, fully traceable.

③  Beyond the brief: an AI feedback loop
INSIGHT

Merchant feedback piled up faster than the team could read it.

SOLUTION

On my own initiative, built an n8n + LLM workflow that classifies feedback by theme each week.

IMPACT

A full day of manual reading replaced by an automated weekly summary.

CHAPTER IV   FUTURE PLAN
04

Start-up idea: TrustLedger

Designing a credit passport for household businesses and small SMEs.

01  Problem

Vietnam's 5.3 million household businesses (GSO, 2026) generate about 30% of GDP, yet formal credit rarely reaches them, and the same is true across Southeast Asia.

Their records are split across many different places, so no single record can prove their business is reliable:

Bank accountsE-invoicesPOS systemsE-commerce platformsPaper notebooks & spreadsheets

When they ask for a loan, they have nothing to show the bank that they are reliable.

02  Insight
OBSERVED IN TWO CONTEXTS
Kiva. As an interpreter for international experts at Kiva, a nonprofit microlending organisation, I saw at first hand the barriers underserved households face in accessing capital.
MB Bank. User interviews with shop owners at MB Innovation Lab revealed the same barrier. They had consistent transaction histories, but none in a form lenders could rely on.

Insight: the data exists, but no one can trust it in its current form. TrustLedger gathers these records into one place and turns them into a credit report that banks can trust.

03  Solution

One ledger. One credit passport.

Collect

Aggregate and standardise multi-source data

Classify

Categorise transactions and monitor cash flow

Record

Generate accounting records

Verify

Issue a credit passport with source-level trust ratings

Data sources
TrustLedger
Credit passport
Lender

An AI-native platform. In every step above, from reading scattered records to classifying transactions and checking how reliable each source is, I see real potential for AI to do the work that small businesses cannot do by themselves.

05STARTUP IDEA

TrustLedger: a credit passport for the shops I met with paper notebooks.

Many small businesses in China and Southeast Asia cannot borrow because they lack collateral and paperwork, even when their payment history says they are reliable. TrustLedger turns e-wallet, transfer and online-sales data into a clear, explainable record that owners control and can share with lenders.
